# On archiving digital media
#### In 25 years, how will you show your descendants photos of your younger self?

For the last couple of years, I've been wondering how people preserve/archive their digital media, how future-proof their methods are, and if the family photo album as we've known it is on its way to dissapear.


Let me ask you a question: Those photos you took 10-15 years ago with your old digicam, the ones from that special trip you did with your partner, the videos of your nephews singing to a song you played on the cassette player when they were 4 y/o. Those small moments that define yourself...

_Do you know where they are now? Will you know where they are in 20 years when your kids ask you about them? Will they be recoverable by then?_

I think that the fact that the digital media does not occupy any physical space makes people not pay attention to them. More over, if we don't pay attention to digital media and where it is, how can we be sure that in 5-10-20 years in the future that media is still there? And especially, will it still be readable?

This idea, of bringing physicality to the digital memories without them leaving the digital realm(you can't really print a video can you), has been fixated on my mind for the past 3 years.

This present article is an experiment on answering this question: **A question of time, legacy, preservation and the will to be remembered.**

### The CD as the physical representation of the digital realm
I was born in 2003. It's rare that I have physical photos of myself(the ones I have are from when I was a toddler and those were likely shot on film), so pretty much every photo of myself afterwards is saved as digital media. Most of them stored on CDs.

Even if the CD is considered an obsolete media storage option nowadays, I do think it still has a ton of value and will still be present in 15 years time. Besides, it has lately become a symbol of ownership as [corporations are ending sales of physical games](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/sony-playstation-physical-discs-digital-2028/) and films.

The CD is quite neat, a disc that can hold digital information in the shape of bumps and troughs by burning a plastic layer with a laser, and reading that information by interpreting the reflected light given those deformations. If stored correctly, a Library Of Congress study shows that [CDs can store information for 20+ years, although hard to estimate given the broad variability of the conditions](https://www.loc.gov/preservation/resources/rt/CDservicelife_rev.pdf).

It is also a physical object, of not too large in size and small and slim enough to be present and not occupy much of your real estate, but it won't be as easy to lose.
